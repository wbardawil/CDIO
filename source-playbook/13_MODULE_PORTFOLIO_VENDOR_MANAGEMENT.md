# MODULE 13: PORTFOLIO & VENDOR MANAGEMENT PLAYBOOK

## 📋 Module Overview

**Based on the CIDO Certificate Program**  
**Module 13: Portfolio and Vendor Management – Driving Execution**  
**Instructor:** Bill Fortwangler, Senior Vice President & CIO, Dollar Bank

### Module Description
This module addresses the critical importance of building a program management culture, establishing robust PMO functions, fixing delivery issues, and implementing effective governance. It covers best practices for managing vendor relationships, IT contract negotiations, and third-party risk management—essential capabilities for organizations executing complex technology portfolios.

### Why This Matters for Fractional CIDO Engagements

Most mid-market organizations struggle with:
- Projects that run over budget or miss deadlines
- Poor visibility into portfolio status and ROI
- Vendor relationships that don't deliver value
- Contract negotiations where vendors have the upper hand
- Lack of standardized delivery processes
- Third-party risks that aren't properly managed

A fractional CIDO brings executive-level portfolio and vendor management expertise without the cost of full-time leadership, establishing governance frameworks and vendor strategies that drive consistent execution.

## 🚀 IMPLEMENTATION PLAYBOOK

### Phase 1: Portfolio Foundation (Months 1-2)

#### Objective
Establish visibility, governance, and basic management capabilities for the project portfolio and vendor relationships.

#### Key Activities

**Week 1-2: Discovery & Assessment**

1. **Project Portfolio Inventory**
   - Identify all active projects and initiatives
   - Document project scope, budget, timeline, and status
   - Assess project health (red/yellow/green)
   - Identify resource constraints and bottlenecks
   - Map project interdependencies

2. **Vendor Landscape Analysis**
   - Create comprehensive vendor inventory
   - Document contract terms, spend, and expiration dates
   - Identify top 20 vendors by spend
   - Assess vendor criticality and performance
   - Map vendor relationships to projects

3. **Current State Assessment**
   - Conduct Module 13 maturity assessment
   - Interview key stakeholders (project managers, business leaders, procurement)
   - Review existing governance processes
   - Analyze historical delivery performance
   - Identify pain points and quick wins

**Week 3-4: Quick Wins**

1. **Project Portfolio Triage**
   - Categorize projects by health status
   - Identify projects requiring immediate attention
   - Create recovery plans for troubled projects
   - Establish weekly project status reviews
   - Set up portfolio dashboard

2. **Vendor Quick Wins**
   - Identify upcoming contract renewals (next 90 days)
   - Create renewal calendar and strategy
   - Identify potential savings opportunities
   - Schedule vendor business reviews
   - Address immediate vendor issues

**Week 5-8: Foundation Building**

1. **Portfolio Governance Framework**
   - Define governance structure and bodies
   - Establish decision rights and escalation paths
   - Create portfolio review cadence
   - Develop standardized reporting templates
   - Implement stage-gate approval process

2. **Vendor Management Framework**
   - Define vendor management approach
   - Create vendor performance scorecard
   - Establish vendor review schedule
   - Develop contract negotiation playbook
   - Set up vendor risk assessment process

#### Deliverables

- Complete project portfolio inventory and dashboard
- Vendor inventory with contract calendar
- Portfolio governance framework document
- Vendor management program charter
- Quick win implementation results
- 90-day roadmap for next phase

#### Success Metrics

- 100% project portfolio visibility achieved
- Portfolio dashboard operational
- All contracts tracked with renewal dates
- Governance meetings scheduled and functioning
- 3-5 quick wins delivered

#### Estimated Fractional CIDO Time

- Weeks 1-2: 20 hours/week (discovery intensive)
- Weeks 3-8: 12-16 hours/week (implementation guidance)
- Total Phase 1: ~100-120 hours over 2 months

## 💡 BEST PRACTICES & LESSONS LEARNED

### Portfolio Management Best Practices

**1. Start with Transparency**
- Create complete project inventory before trying to manage it
- Use simple dashboards with red/yellow/green status
- Make portfolio status visible to all stakeholders
- Don't hide troubled projects – address them directly
- Establish trust through honest, consistent reporting

**2. Governance Must Have Teeth**
- Decision rights must be clear and respected
- Executives must actively participate, not just attend
- Governance bodies must make actual decisions, not just review
- Stage-gate approvals must be enforced, not bypassed
- Consequences needed for projects that don't follow process

**3. Resource Management is Critical**
- Resource constraints cause more project failures than budget issues
- Track resources realistically (people are not 100% available)
- Address resource conflicts at portfolio level
- Use resource capacity planning to prevent overcommitment
- Build in buffer for unplanned work and support

**4. Value Must Be Tracked**
- Define expected business value for every project
- Track value realization, not just project completion
- Hold project sponsors accountable for benefits
- Kill projects that won't deliver value
- Communicate value achieved to build credibility

**5. PMO Must Serve the Business**
- PMO exists to help projects succeed, not create bureaucracy
- Tailor processes to project size and risk
- Focus on value-add activities, not paperwork
- Build relationships with project sponsors and teams
- Continuously improve based on feedback

### Vendor Management Best Practices

**1. Vendor Relationships are Strategic**
- Treat vendors as partners, not just suppliers
- Invest in relationship building with key vendors
- Establish executive-level vendor relationships
- Have regular business reviews, not just problem calls
- Look for innovation opportunities with vendors

**2. Contract Negotiation Preparation Wins**
- Start renewal discussions 12+ months early
- Do market research on pricing and alternatives
- Document vendor performance issues objectively
- Have real alternatives or be prepared to walk away
- Use multi-year commitments to get better terms

**3. Third-Party Risk is Real**
- Vendor breaches can destroy your organization
- Risk assessments must be thorough and ongoing
- Don't accept vendor security questionnaires at face value
- Verify critical vendors through audits
- Have vendor incident response plans ready

**4. Vendor Portfolio Optimization Saves Millions**
- Vendor sprawl is expensive and risky
- Consolidate vendors where it makes sense
- Negotiate enterprise agreements to reduce costs
- Eliminate redundant or underutilized vendors
- Use vendor spending analytics to find opportunities

**5. Contract Terms Matter**
- Don't accept vendor standard terms
- Negotiate termination rights and data portability
- Ensure audit rights and performance guarantees
- Watch out for auto-renewals and price escalators
- Involve legal early in contract negotiations

### Common Pitfalls to Avoid

**Portfolio Management Pitfalls**

1. **Analysis Paralysis**
   - Don't wait for perfect data to start managing the portfolio
   - Start with 80% visibility and improve iteratively
   - Make decisions with available information

2. **Too Much Governance**
   - Don't create governance processes that slow everything down
   - Right-size governance to project size and risk
   - Reduce approval layers where possible

3. **PMO Seen as Bureaucracy**
   - Don't implement processes for process sake
   - Focus on value-add activities
   - Get feedback and adjust quickly

4. **Resource Over-Allocation**
   - Don't assume people can work 100% on projects
   - Factor in support, meetings, and unplanned work
   - Say no to new projects when resources are constrained

5. **Ignoring Organizational Change**
   - Don't underestimate the change management needed
   - Invest in communication and training
   - Build coalitions with business leaders

**Vendor Management Pitfalls**

1. **Vendor Lock-In**
   - Don't let vendors become impossible to replace
   - Maintain data portability and exit rights
   - Periodically assess alternatives

2. **Relationship Complacency**
   - Don't let vendor relationships get stale
   - Don't accept poor performance as normal
   - Regularly assess whether vendor still fits

3. **Contract Auto-Renewals**
   - Don't let contracts auto-renew without negotiation
   - Set calendar reminders 12+ months before renewal
   - Challenge price increases and demand concessions

4. **Inadequate Due Diligence**
   - Don't skip vendor risk assessments
   - Don't accept vendor assurances without verification
   - Do thorough reference checks

5. **Shadow IT Vendors**
   - Don't allow departments to engage vendors independently
   - Enforce procurement and vendor approval processes
   - Track all vendor relationships centrally

### A. Glossary of Terms

**Portfolio Management:**
- **Portfolio:** Collection of projects, programs, and operations managed as a group
- **PMO:** Project Management Office - function providing project management support
- **Governance:** Framework for decision-making and oversight
- **Stage-Gate:** Approval process with defined decision points
- **Resource Capacity:** Available effort from resources for project work
- **Value Realization:** Achievement of expected business benefits

**Vendor Management:**
- **Strategic Vendor:** Vendor critical to business operations or strategy
- **Tactical Vendor:** Vendor providing commodity or standard services
- **SLA:** Service Level Agreement - performance commitments
- **MSA:** Master Services Agreement - overarching contract terms
- **SOW:** Statement of Work - specific project or service description
- **TCO:** Total Cost of Ownership - all costs associated with vendor relationship
